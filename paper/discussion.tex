Three findings survive scrutiny. First, a modest 1-D convolutional network
over one-hot plus physicochemical encodings is a strong AMP recognizer under
leakage-controlled evaluation (AUROC $0.921$ internal, $0.926$ external on
APD6), and it scales where composition baselines saturate. Second, the chain
graph network underperforms the CNN at every scale we tested; without
contact-map or structure-derived edges, message passing along the backbone
adds parameters and optimization difficulty without adding information the
convolutions lack. We keep the GNN in the paper as a negative architectural
result. Third, random-split benchmarks in this domain are optimistically
biased: the same model family scored $\sim$0.95 AUROC under naive
cross-validation on a small corpus and $0.92$ under cluster-aware splitting
on a corpus $18\times$ larger, and the relative ranking of baselines changed
with the split discipline.

Limitations are real. The negative class is ``not annotated antimicrobial in
UniProt,'' which is not the same as experimentally non-antimicrobial; some
fraction of negatives are surely unrecognized AMPs, biasing all reported
metrics conservatively. The training distribution is dominated by short
cationic peptides, so APD6 families outside that mold (anionic AMPs, longer
disulfide-stabilized peptides) are systematically underscored, as the 0.601
sensitivity at 5\% FPR quantifies. The annealed designs and screen hits carry
no experimental validation; their novelty scores are sequence-level, not
structural. DBAASP was unreachable programmatically during this work (its
full-dataset endpoint returns an empty body without an interactive session),
so the APD6 list served as the sole external benchmark; adding DBAASP's
activity-annotated records is the natural next validation. Finally, the
sandbox budget (2 CPU, 2 GB RAM) capped model size, epochs, and the training
subsample; larger models on the full 40k train split would likely improve the
numbers, but not the conclusions.

Verdict: PepCNN is a solid, honestly-evaluated AMP classifier with verified
cross-database transfer, and the annealing designer plus discovery screen are
useful prioritization tools. Nothing here is a claimed therapeutic or a
benchmark record; the contribution is a leakage-controlled pipeline, an
external-validation result, and a set of ranked hypotheses.

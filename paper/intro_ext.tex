\subsection{Biological setting}
AMPs are produced across all domains of life. The canonical family --- short,
net-cationic, amphipathic peptides such as magainins, LL-37, and the
defensins --- partitions into microbial membranes and kills by
permeabilization, but the known universe also includes anionic AMPs,
glycine-rich peptides, proline-rich peptides that enter cells and inhibit
translation, and larger disulfide-stabilized folds. This mechanistic variety
is the central recognition challenge: there is no single sequence signature,
and any single-feature rule (charge, hydrophobic moment, length) fails on
whole families. It is also why we treat sensitivity numbers honestly: a model
trained on a corpus dominated by short cationic peptides should not be
expected to score disulfide-locked anionic families highly, and ours does not
(Section 7.3).

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A fully provenance-logged, leakage-controlled AMP benchmark:
24{,}628 reviewed UniProt KW-0929 positives against 24{,}628 length-matched
reviewed negatives, clustered by shared exact 10-mers into 12{,}110
components and split 80/10/10 by cluster, with all fetch URLs, hashes, and
timestamps recorded.
\item A paired comparison of two neural architectures and two composition
baselines under this split, with bootstrap intervals and permutation tests
computed by one shared harness: PepCNN attains AUROC $0.921$ [0.913, 0.929],
significantly ahead of all baselines; the graph model is a reported negative
result.
\item External validation on the APD6 2024 natural AMP list after strict
decontamination: PepCNN transfers at AUROC $0.926$ [0.918, 0.934], with
sensitivity $0.601$ at a 5\% false-positive rate --- evidence of real
cross-database generalization and an honest bound on its reach.
\item A provably correct Metropolis--Hastings designer
(Theorem~\ref{thm:mh}) producing 24 ranked novel candidates, and a discovery
screen over 852 reviewed uncharacterized proteins producing 25 ranked
hypotheses, all clearly labeled as in-silico.
\item A reproducible software artifact: the \texttt{pepdesign} package, four
study scripts, a 21-test hermetic suite, and regeneration of every table and
figure in this paper from checked-in result files.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scope}
This paper is item 9 part 1 of the MEGA-Program-27 series and is deliberately
bounded: recognition, design, and screening of AMPs. Activity prediction
against specific strains, toxicity estimation, secondary-structure
validation, and wet-lab follow-up are out of scope and are the natural
subjects of subsequent items.
